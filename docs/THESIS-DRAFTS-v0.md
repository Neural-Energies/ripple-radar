## Spine (from CEO Office)

A shock is not a ticker. The work is the ripple — who moves, through what bottleneck, what kills it — on a multi-day horizon. News stops at the headline; Terminals are the kitchen; neither builds event → graph → exposures. Importance ≠ probability.

---

## Draft A — Slogan / thesis (short)

**Don't trade the headline. Trade what it causes next.**

A headline is a spark. The desk's job is the second- and third-order book: transmission, bottlenecks, invalidation — not the first obvious ticker.

---

## Draft B — Why headline → ticker fails

Headline → ticker collapses research into a reflex. It skips the causal map: who is exposed through which channel, what is crowded vs confirmed, what would falsify the path. Speed to the obvious name is not the same as speed to the book. The failure mode is structural blindness dressed up as decisiveness.

---

## Draft C — Importance ≠ probability

Not every high-probability path is the one that matters for the book, and not every important exposure is the one with a neat number. Ranking by costume confidence trains desks to confuse calibration theater with transmission. Importance is about causal distance and what breaks if the path is wrong — not a single %.

---

## Draft D — Structural / multi-day vs millisecond arb

Millisecond and same-session arb live on being first to the headline. Structural edge lives on understanding what the shock *causes* over days: bottlenecks, second-order names, what invalidates. Racing the tape and building the ripple are different jobs. Confusing them produces the wrong tool and the wrong buyer.
